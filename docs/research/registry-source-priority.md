# State data broker registry source priority

**Reviewed:** 2026-10-03  
**Scope:** Official registry pages, statutes, forms, and government guides for California, Vermont, Texas, and Oregon. This is a source-access assessment, not legal advice or a data license determination.

## Recommendation

Prioritize California for a future ingestion adapter because its registry offers a bulk registration-information download and documents useful response fields. Treat Vermont and Texas as manual research sources until a supported bulk access method is found. Oregon is valuable for consumer workflow research, but its official lookup requires interactive CAPTCHA validation.

**Do not persist ingested registry records or publish derived broker records until the source terms and reuse permission have been recorded.** A downloadable or searchable public registry does not by itself grant permission to mirror or redistribute its data. No explicit reuse license was located on the reviewed registry pages.

## Bounded next step

Before implementation, record the applicable source terms and reuse decision for the intended data and publication model. The October 3, 2026 review below found no registry-specific republication grant for any of these sources. Access through a public registry or public-records law is not recorded as a reuse license. Then verify California’s current download URL and CSV headers manually. If reuse is approved, assign one California-only adapter against the approved shared ingestion contract, with local synthetic fixtures and no network-dependent tests. Preserve source version, retrieval time, and evidence; treat disclosures as broker-reported. Do not use DROP consumer request data as a registry source.

### Project decision

- Keep the public Texas additions limited to first-party broker request-path evidence. Those cards do not claim that a broker is registered in Texas.
- Do not persist state-registry submissions, narratives, or derived broker records yet. The exact intended publication scope and any third-party rights need a documented decision for each source.
- For a future source inquiry, ask the registry owner whether Unlisted may retain and republish individual fields and broker-written narratives, whether there are limits on commercial/public redistribution, and whether an official machine-readable feed has terms distinct from the web portal. No inquiry has been sent.
- Human verification can proceed separately from data reuse review. For Vermont and Oregon, capture only the minimal fields needed for a candidate review after a person passes the ordinary validation gate; record the date and link back to the state record. Do not automate or bypass that gate.

## Suggested parallel work packages

Keep these as separate tasks with disjoint deliverables:

1. **Source terms review:** document official terms or seek clarification for the intended California fields and public display model. No network ingestion or database changes. The connector remains blocked until this gate is resolved.
2. **California adapter:** after the terms decision, own only a California source module and synthetic parser tests. Do not change shared contracts or migrations; follow the existing `SourceConnector` interface and preserve a review-required source status unless the reuse decision explicitly approves persistence.
3. **Human-assisted state samples:** inspect selected Vermont, Texas, and Oregon records only through their supported interactive search flows, then compare declared routes with first-party privacy pages. Record evidence, and do not automate through CAPTCHA.
4. **Entity-resolution design:** once at least two compatible source datasets are approved, draft matching signals and ambiguity review rules in a design note. Keep canonical identity contracts and migrations with one integrator until the source evidence is available.
5. **Private request tracking:** defer implementation until authentication, user-data retention, authorization, and account-security requirements are designed separately from the public workflow catalog.
